Key Questions Before Choosing a Broker

Begin by clarifying payment structures and the scope of services. The Bendigo guide indicates that initial consultations are free and that Australian residential brokers typically receive commission from lenders, with any exceptions disclosed upfront as per ASIC regulations. Enquire about the disclosure of this remuneration, the range of lenders considered, and the rationale behind the recommended loan option aligning with your circumstances. Before commencing an application, seek clear answers regarding:

  • Which lenders were evaluated for your specific scenario, and why were others excluded?
  • Is the proposed loan structure fixed, variable, split, interest-only, or principal and interest?
  • What documentation is required to obtain a reliable borrowing estimate?
  • What assumptions are being made about your income, existing debts, deposit amount, and the property's valuation?
  • What factors could potentially delay the assessment or settlement process after the application is lodged?

The source material notes that partner brokers must hold an Australian Credit Licence or operate under one. It also mentions the broker's best interests duty and the availability of external dispute resolution through AFCA. While these protections are valuable, borrowers should still request clear, written justifications before selecting a loan product.

Timing, Pre-Approval, and Settlement

Pre-approval is defined as conditional approval that helps estimate a lender's potential loan amount before you begin searching for a property. According to the Bendigo guide, this typically remains valid for approximately 90 days, varying by lender. It should be viewed as a planning instrument rather than a final loan commitment for any specific property. The process from application to settlement is generally estimated to take about 4 to 6 weeks, contingent on the lender and individual circumstances. Clarify the specific activities involved during this period, such as document collection, lender assessment, property valuation (if necessary), loan offer review, signing of agreements, and coordination of settlement. If your purchase or refinance has a strict deadline, ascertain which tasks are your responsibility. Providing complete payslips, bank statements, identification, and details of current liabilities promptly can expedite the process and minimise back-and-forth communication. A broker should clearly explain when a loan offer is conditional, when it becomes final, and what circumstances might necessitate a reassessment.

Comparing Loan Structures

Loan comparison should be driven by the primary purpose of the loan. A first-time buyer might prioritise certainty and eligibility criteria. A borrower looking to refinance may focus on repayment amounts, associated fees, debt consolidation opportunities, and the utility of an offset account. Investors often need to compare cash flow projections, the utilisation of equity, and the differences between interest-only and principal-and-interest repayment structures. The Bendigo source material specifically references variable versus fixed rates, offset accounts, interest-only versus principal-and-interest options, and the use of existing equity. Each of these features can be advantageous or disadvantageous depending on the borrower's financial behaviour and goals. A lower advertised interest rate may not be the most suitable option if the loan's structure, fees, or conditions do not align with your objectives. Request that the broker explain not only the recommended loan but also any options that were rejected and the reasons for their rejection. Clear explanations for declined options can often indicate whether a recommendation is based on lender policies, serviceability constraints, specific loan features, or timing considerations. Clearly define your goal - whether it's buying, refinancing, investing, or seeking pre-approval - before engaging in comparisons with lenders.
